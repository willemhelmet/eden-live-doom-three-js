# A real Deck, a real game, a different world

The hook is the physical interaction: your hands on the Deck, an ordinary Doom view, then one press of Y changes the world while you continue moving. The screen should be legible and the controller visible in the same shot.

## A 20–30 second take

1. Establish the Deck over your shoulder. Keep the screen nearly square to camera, lower room lighting and lock camera exposure/focus to the display. Avoid reflections. Test shutter speed against the Deck refresh rate to avoid banding.
2. Start Eden off camera, wait for the generated view, then use Y to return to the original. Find a clear angle and keep the camera still for a moment. This avoids spending the opening of the post on connection UI.
3. Roll on original Doom for 2–3 seconds, moving slowly. Press Y with your thumb visibly in frame to reveal Eden. Continue a smooth walk or turn for 8–12 seconds. Open a door or fire once so it is clearly interactive.
4. Press Y again briefly to show the same underlying game, or View for a split view. End while the generation still looks strong. Start a fresh connection for another take rather than stitching over a failed live segment.
5. Optionally add one clean screen recording as a cut-in, retaining the over-the-shoulder shot as the primary evidence. Label any cuts or accelerated wait instead of implying instant startup.

Record several short honest takes and pick the best coherent run. X stops Eden; Restart level resets the underlying game before another setup. An art-directed appearance reference matching the opening camera is worth preparing before filming.

## Caption draft

> Doom, but the world is rendered live by AI.
>
> Playing on my Steam Deck: Three.js runs the game locally, Reactor's EdenLive transforms the visuals in the cloud. Same controls, enemies and collisions. Press a button to see the original.
>
> Code + setup: https://github.com/willemhelmet/eden-live-doom-three-js

Keep the claim precise: the Deck runs the game and displays the stream; the model is not running on the Deck. Only call the take live after the cloud connection and physical controls have actually been checked. No performance numbers are implied by this caption.
